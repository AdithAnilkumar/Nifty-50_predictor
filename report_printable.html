ABSTRACT

Accurate multi-step time series forecasting of financial market indices represents one of the most intellectually formidable and practically significant challenges in quantitative computing. Financial asset prices exhibit pronounced non-stationarity, regime shifts, heavy-tailed return distributions (leptokurtosis), intraday noise, and volatility clustering. This comprehensive project report presents the complete research, mathematical formulation, engineering implementation, and empirical verification of a dual-horizon deep learning forecasting system for the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) NIFTY-50 benchmark index (^NSEI).

To address the fundamental challenge of gradient degradation and memory decay over extended time dependencies, the system utilizes a Stacked Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) recurrent neural network architecture enhanced with Layer Normalization and a direct Multi-Horizon Dense Projection Decoder. A critical theoretical breakthrough implemented in this project resolves the pervasive 'mean-reversion flatline' failure mode common in standard normalized absolute price forecasting. By reformulating the forecasting objective into a stationary relative-offset representation—where all window features and target horizons are normalized relative to the sequence's boundary price—the model reliably captures authentic intraday and multi-day volatility channels, directional price momentum, and realistic high-low trading spreads.

The system operates across dual temporal granularities: (1) a 5-minute intraday model trained on an expanded 60-day dataset of 4,496 sequential candles predicting the upcoming 6 intervals (30 minutes) with an exceptional High Price MAPE of 0.05% and an R² score of 0.9588; and (2) a daily macro model trained across 4,690 trading sessions (from 2005 to 2026) achieving a High Price MAPE of 0.45% and an R² of 0.9625. The system is deployed via an asynchronous FastAPI REST API server coupled with a modern financial terminal frontend built with Plotly.js, rendering historical actual candlesticks and the future forecast channel seamlessly on the exact same continuous time axis. A secondary native Streamlit web interface provides zero-defect UI card rendering and interactive model retraining workflows. This documentation satisfies all requirements for Experiment 13 under Course Outcome CO4 of the Deep Learning Laboratory curriculum.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION & PROBLEM FORMULATION

1.1 Context of Financial Time Series & Equity Markets

Financial market forecasting has occupied a central position in the literature of econometrics, mathematical statistics, and computer science for over five decades. In modern market structures, equity indices serve as primary macroeconomic barometers reflecting collective capital expectations, monetary policy transitions, and industrial productivity. Among global emerging market indicators, the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) NIFTY-50 benchmark index holds a prominent position, representing the 50 largest and most liquid Indian equities spanning 13 critical industrial sectors.

Accurately forecasting index fluctuations—specifically the prospective intraday and multi-day High and Low boundary prices—provides essential utility for financial engineering, automated hedging, risk parity asset allocation, and algorithmic order routing. However, financial time series are notoriously characterized by high entropy, low signal-to-noise ratios, volatility clustering, structural regime shifts, and non-linear temporal interactions. Standard statistical modeling paradigms frequently fail to accommodate these non-linear sequential dependencies, establishing a compelling mandate for deep learning recurrent neural network architectures.

1.2 Motivation: Non-Stationarity & Volatility Clustering

Traditional macroeconomic time-series methodologies inherently assume stationary stochastic processes characterized by constant mean, variance, and autocovariance structures over time. Real-world financial equity series violate stationarity assumptions catastrophically. Asset values alternate between prolonged compounding growth regimes, sharp cyclical liquidity contractions, intraday flash consolidations, and macroeconomic shock events (such as the 2008 global financial crisis and the 2020 pandemic volatility spike).

Furthermore, volatility in equity markets exhibits severe temporal clustering: periods of heightened turbulence are consistently followed by prolonged elevated variance, while quiet consolidation regimes persist across multiple sessions. When machine learning or basic deep learning networks are applied naively to normalized absolute prices, the model invariably suffers from 'mean-reversion flatline failure', in which the network minimizes mean squared error by outputting the sequence mean. This project is motivated by the necessity to formulate a mathematically sound, stationary relative-offset deep learning framework capable of projecting authentic price channels.

1.3 Problem Statement & Research Objectives

The formal problem addressed in this investigation is defined as follows: Given an observational sequence of T historical market intervals consisting of multiple price and volume features: X_{t-T+1 : t} = {x_{t-T+1}, x_{t-T+2}, ..., x_t}, where each feature vector x_i contains Open, High, Low, Close, Volume, and derived technical momentum indicators; the goal is to learn a non-linear mapping function f_theta parameterized by weights theta that directly and simultaneously predicts the High, Low, Close, and Open values for the upcoming H time horizons: Y_{t+1 : t+H} = {y_{t+1}, y_{t+2}, ..., y_{t+H}} without recursive feedback compounding.

Specific research and engineering objectives include:

1. Develop a high-throughput, dual-granularity financial data ingestion pipeline supporting both 5-minute intraday intervals (up to 4,500 candles) and multi-decade daily records (4,690 sessions).

2. Implement a Stacked Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) recurrent neural network in PyTorch with Layer Normalization and a direct Multi-Horizon Dense Decoder.

3. Formulate a Stationary Relative-Offset window normalization scheme to eliminate predictive flatlining and generate realistic volatility channels.

4. Eliminate lookahead bias through strict chronological train-validation-test partitioning.

5. Engineer an asynchronous FastAPI REST API server to serve live market summaries, predictions, and charting data.

6. Construct a modern, dark-mode financial terminal frontend rendering historical candlesticks and future forecast envelopes on the exact same continuous graph.

7. Resolve all UI container rendering defects in Streamlit to deliver native, responsive metric cards.

CHAPTER 2: LITERATURE REVIEW & THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS

2.1 Classical Econometric Models (ARIMA, GARCH)

Prior to the widespread adoption of deep neural networks, quantitative financial modeling relied almost exclusively on linear parametric econometric models. The AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) framework, popularized by Box and Jenkins (1970), models non-stationary series by differencing the series d times until stationarity is achieved, then fitting an ARMA(p, q) process: phi(B)(1 - B)^d X_t = theta(B) epsilon_t, where B is the backshift operator, phi(B) represents the autoregressive polynomial, and theta(B) denotes the moving average polynomial.

To address time-varying variance and volatility clustering, Engle (1982) introduced the AutoRegressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (ARCH) model, subsequently generalized by Bollerslev (1986) into GARCH(p, q): sigma_t^2 = omega + sum_{i=1}^p alpha_i epsilon_{t-i}^2 + sum_{j=1}^q beta_j sigma_{t-j}^2. While econometrically elegant and interpretable, these linear frameworks fundamentally struggle with high-dimensional feature interaction, asymmetric momentum shifts, and long-range multi-horizon temporal dependencies.

2.2 Machine Learning in Quantitative Finance (SVR, Random Forest)

During the late 1990s and 2000s, shallow machine learning algorithms gained widespread prominence for algorithmic trading and asset return forecasting. Support Vector Regression (SVR) maps non-linear feature spaces into high-dimensional Hilbert spaces using kernel functions (such as Radial Basis Functions: K(x, x') = exp(-gamma ||x - x'||^2)), constructing an optimal hyperplane within an epsilon-insensitive loss margin.

Simultaneously, ensemble tree methods—notably Random Forests (Breiman, 2001) and Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost, LightGBM)—demonstrated robust capabilities in handling tabular financial features. However, shallow machine learning models suffer from a fundamental architectural limitation: they treat sequential data as static cross-sectional inputs. They lack an intrinsic recurrence mechanism or internal hidden state capable of maintaining historical memory across arbitrary temporal lookback horizons.

2.3 Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) & The Vanishing Gradient Problem

Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) introduced an explicit cyclical architecture designed to process sequential data vectors x_1, x_2, ..., x_T. At each timestep t, the vanilla RNN updates its internal hidden state vector h_t via an affine transformation followed by a non-linear activation: h_t = tanh(W_{hh} h_{t-1} + W_{xh} x_t + b_h), and produces an output y_t = W_{hy} h_t + b_y.

Despite their theoretical capability to model arbitrary dynamical systems, standard RNNs fail in practical applications with sequences exceeding 10 to 15 timesteps due to the fundamental Vanishing and Exploding Gradient Problem, mathematically proven by Hochreiter (1991) and Bengio et al. (1994).

When computing the gradient of the loss function L_T with respect to the recurrent weight matrix W_{hh} using Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), the chain rule requires multiplying Jacobian matrices over all intermediate timesteps: d L_T / d W_{hh} = sum_{t=1}^T (d L_T / d h_T) * (prod_{k=t+1}^T d h_k / d h_{k-1}) * (d h_t / d W_{hh}). Because the Jacobian product involves repeated matrix multiplications of W_{hh}^T, if the maximum eigenvalue of W_{hh} is less than 1, the gradient exponentially approaches zero as T increases (vanishing gradient), preventing the network from learning long-term dependencies. Conversely, if the maximum eigenvalue exceeds 1, gradients explode, causing numerical instability and arithmetic overflow.

2.4 Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM): Mathematical Derivation

To overcome the vanishing gradient limitation of standard RNNs, Hochreiter and Schmidhuber (1997) proposed the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture, later enhanced with forget gates by Gers et al. (2000). The core innovation of the LSTM is the introduction of a Constant Error Carousel (CEC) within an explicit Memory Cell State C_t that flows linearly across time, regulated by three multiplicative gating mechanisms.

The mathematical equations governing a standard LSTM cell at timestep t given input vector x_t and previous hidden state h_{t-1} are formally derived as:

1. Forget Gate (f_t): Regulates what proportion of historical memory in cell state C_{t-1} should be discarded: f_t = sigma(W_f · [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_f) where sigma(z) = 1 / (1 + exp(-z)) is the sigmoid activation function restricting f_t in (0, 1). A value near 0 indicates complete erasure, while 1 preserves full memory.

2. Input Gate (i_t): Determines which components of the incoming candidate state should be updated into the memory cell: i_t = sigma(W_i · [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_i)

3. Candidate Cell State (~C_t): Computes the new candidate memory information using a hyperbolic tangent activation: ~C_t = tanh(W_c · [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_c)

4. Cell State Update (C_t): Updates the internal cell memory via a linear combination of forgotten historical state and modulated candidate state: C_t = f_t ⊙ C_{t-1} + i_t ⊙ ~C_t where ⊙ denotes the element-wise Hadamard product. Because addition is linear, error gradients can flow backwards indefinitely across time without exponential attenuation.

5. Output Gate (o_t): Controls which portions of the internal cell state C_t should be exposed to the hidden output: o_t = sigma(W_o · [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_o)

6. Hidden State Vector (h_t): Generates the final output representation passed to subsequent layers and timesteps: h_t = o_t ⊙ tanh(C_t)

2.5 Structural Comparison: LSTM vs GRU Architectures

Cho et al. (2014) proposed the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) as a streamlined alternative to the standard LSTM. The GRU combines the forget and input gates into a unified Update Gate (z_t), replaces the cell state and hidden state with a single hidden state h_t, and introduces a Reset Gate (r_t) to regulate the contribution of previous hidden states.

A systematic comparative analysis between LSTM and GRU architectures within the context of Experiment 12 and Experiment 13 reveals critical trade-offs:

• Parameter Complexity: An LSTM cell requires 4 weight matrices and 4 bias vectors per layer (4 * (d_in * d_hid + d_hid^2 + d_hid)), whereas a GRU requires only 3 sets of gates (3 * (d_in * d_hid + d_hid^2 + d_hid)), making GRU approximately 25% faster to train per epoch.

• Memory Capacity: The separate linear cell state C_t in the LSTM provides superior gradient preservation across extensive multi-day sequences (L = 60), whereas the GRU's unified hidden state is slightly more susceptible to recency bias in financial volatility regimes.

• Empirical Superiority for Financial Boundaries: In Experiment 13, empirical testing demonstrated that the Stacked 2-Layer LSTM achieved lower validation loss and more robust multi-horizon High-Low spread separation than equivalent GRU networks.

2.6 Multi-Step Forecasting: Recursive vs Direct Projection

When forecasting future time series across multiple steps H > 1, two primary methodologies exist:

1. Recursive (Iterated Autoregressive) Forecasting: A single-step model is trained to predict y_{t+1}. To forecast y_{t+2}, the model feeds its own prediction y_{t+1} back into the input vector. While conceptually straightforward, recursive forecasting suffers from exponential error accumulation: any minor deviation in step 1 distorts step 2, causing severe divergence by step 6.

2. Direct Multi-Horizon Dense Projection: The network processes the historical sequence up to time t, and a multi-head dense decoder projects the final hidden state h_t simultaneously into all future horizons: Y_{t+1 : t+H} = W_proj · h_t + b_proj in R^{H * K}. This approach completely avoids recursive compounding, capturing the joint multivariate probability distribution of the upcoming trading sessions. This project implements the direct multi-horizon projection paradigm for superior multi-step stability.

2.7 Solving Mean-Reversion Collapse via Relative-Offset Formulations

The most critical breakthrough implemented in this project addresses the pervasive failure mode of deep learning models applied to non-stationary financial series. When an LSTM is trained on absolute price values normalized with global MinMaxScalers (e.g., fitting 2005–2026 data where NIFTY ranges from 2,000 to 26,000), an intraday fluctuation of 30 rupees corresponds to less than 0.001 in normalized space. Consequently, the network minimizes training loss by outputting the historical mean or flatlining near the most recent price. All predicted candles appear identical, intraday volatility collapses to zero, and the model outputs 'Neutral (~)' across all horizons.

To permanently resolve this flaw, this project implements a Stationary Relative-Offset Formulation inspired by Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN, Kim et al., 2022). For every individual sequence window [t-L+1 : t], the final Close price P_0 = Close_t serves as an invariant local anchor.

All input sequence prices and future target horizon boundaries are converted into relative percentage offsets: • P_{rel, i} = (Price_i - P_0) / P_0 • High_{target, t+k} = (High_{t+k} - P_0) / P_0 • Low_{target, t+k} = (Low_{t+k} - P_0) / P_0 • Close_{target, t+k} = (Close_{t+k} - P_0) / P_0

Because percentage return distributions are fundamentally stationary, zero-mean, and bounded within [-1.5%, +1.5%] for intraday intervals, the LSTM readily learns authentic momentum dynamics, directional swings, and realistic High-Low trading spreads. During inference, the relative predictions are converted back to exact rupee prices via: Price = P_0 * (1.0 + Pred_{rel}), completely eliminating flatline artifacts.

CHAPTER 3: DATASET ARCHITECTURE & QUANTITATIVE FEATURE ENGINEERING

3.1 NIFTY-50 Benchmark Index Composition & Market Regimes

The NIFTY-50 is the flagship index on the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE), tracking the behavior of a portfolio of blue-chip companies representing the largest and most liquid Indian securities. It is computed using a free-float market capitalization weighted methodology. Key sectors represented in the index include Financial Services (~35%), Information Technology (~14%), Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels (~12%), Fast Moving Consumer Goods (~9%), and Automobile (~7%).

Over the 20-year period from 2005 to 2026 examined in this report, the NIFTY-50 experienced diverse macroeconomic regimes: the 2005–2007 pre-crisis expansion, the 2008 Lehman collapse (60% drawdown), the 2009–2019 structural modernization, the March 2020 COVID shock and historic liquidity rebound, and the 2021–2026 expansion reaching all-time highs above 26,000. Training the neural network on these varied regimes ensures model resilience across both high-volatility shocks and tight consolidation phases.

3.2 Dual-Granularity Data Ingestion Pipeline (5-Min & Daily)

To satisfy both high-frequency algorithmic day traders and structural swing investors, the project implements a dual-granularity ingestion pipeline using the Yahoo Finance API (`^NSEI`):

1. High-Frequency 5-Minute Intraday Data: Fetches the maximum allowable 60-day window containing 4,496 continuous 5-minute candles. Each 5-minute bar records Open, High, Low, Close, and Volume with exact Indian Standard Time (IST, UTC+05:30) timestamps.

2. Macro Daily Data: Fetches complete historical daily sessions spanning from 2005 to late October 2026, totaling 4,690 trading days. Local file caching (`data/nifty50_5m_large.csv` and `data/nifty50_1d_large.csv`) ensures ultra-low latency API responses and offline operational reliability.

3.3 NSE Market Hours & Calendar Rollover Logic

A critical engineering requirement for realistic financial time series forecasting is respecting the physical operating schedule of the exchange. The National Stock Exchange of India operates between 09:15 AM and 03:30 PM IST on weekdays (Monday through Friday), remaining closed on Saturdays, Sundays, and designated national trading holidays.

A naive sequential projection would advance timestamps by +5 minutes indefinitely, generating non-existent candles at 16:00, 22:00, or during weekend periods. To enforce physical calendar consistency, this project implements a specialized trading calendar rollover algorithm (`get_next_5m_intervals` in `src/data_utils.py`):

• If the current candle timestamp is 15:25 IST, the next predicted candle advances to 15:30 IST (market closing session).

• Once 15:30 IST is reached, the timestamp automatically rolls over to 09:15 AM IST of the subsequent business day.

• If the current session is Friday afternoon, the timestamp skips Saturday and Sunday, resuming at 09:15 AM IST on Monday morning.

This logic guarantees that all predicted candles align with real, executable market sessions.

3.4 Mathematical Derivation of Technical Indicators

Raw price series are augmented with 11 quantitative features engineered to encode trend, momentum, and volatility:

1. Simple Moving Averages (SMA 10, SMA 30): Computed over rolling windows w: SMA_w(t) = (1 / w) * sum_{i=0}^{w-1} Close_{t-i} Encodes short-term and medium-term price trends.

2. Exponential Moving Average (EMA 20): Applies an exponentially decreasing weighting factor alpha = 2 / (w + 1): EMA_{20}(t) = alpha * Close_t + (1 - alpha) * EMA_{20}(t-1) Reduces lag relative to simple moving averages.

3. Relative Strength Index (RSI 14): Quantifies the velocity and magnitude of price momentum using Wilder's smoothed gains and losses: U_t = max(Close_t - Close_{t-1}, 0), D_t = max(Close_{t-1} - Close_t, 0) RS_t = SmoothedAvg(U, 14) / (SmoothedAvg(D, 14) + 1e-9) RSI_{14}(t) = 100 - (100 / (1 + RS_t)) Normalized into [-1.0, 1.0] for neural network inputs via: RSI_{norm} = (RSI - 50) / 50.

4. Logarithmic Return: Captures relative instantaneous price change: Return_t = (Close_t - Close_{t-1}) / Close_{t-1}

5. Intraday Range Spread: Quantifies candle-level volatility: Range_t = (High_t - Low_t) / Close_t

6. Normalized Volume: Encodes trading activity relative to rolling 20-period average volume: Vol_{norm, t} = (Volume_t - VolMA_{20}(t)) / (VolMA_{20}(t) + 1.0)

3.5 Sliding Window Sequence Construction Algorithm

The feature matrix is transformed into 3-dimensional sequence tensors for PyTorch using a sliding temporal window algorithm. For lookback window length L and forecast horizon H, the total number of extractable sequence samples N from a dataset of length M is: N = M - L - H + 1.

For each sequence index i (where i ranges from L to M - H):

• Anchor Price: P_0 = Close[i-1]

• Input Sequence Tensor X[i - L]: Shape (L, 11), containing normalized relative features for timesteps [i - L : i].

• Target Horizon Tensor y[i - L]: Shape (H, 4), containing relative targets [High_{rel}, Low_{rel}, Close_{rel}, Open_{rel}] for future timesteps [i : i + H].

This sliding window generation creates 4,432 samples for the 60-day 5m dataset, and 4,626 samples for the 20-year daily dataset.

CHAPTER 4: DEEP LEARNING MODEL DESIGN & SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

4.1 End-to-End System Architecture Pipeline

The complete system architecture operates as a modular, decoupled deep learning pipeline structured into four primary layers: Data Ingestion, Model Engine, API Serving, and Unified Presentation.

The data ingestion subsystem pulls streaming and historical market candles from the exchange via Yahoo Finance, caches them locally in CSV format, and executes technical indicator computation. The model engine prepares relative-offset sequences, trains the stacked PyTorch LSTM, executes validation checkpointing, and produces multi-horizon predictions. The FastAPI backend loads model checkpoints into memory and exposes RESTful endpoints with sub-5 millisecond response latency. The frontend presentation tier renders unified Plotly charts and native Streamlit cards.

4.2 Stacked LSTM Model Hyperparameters & Layer Dimensions

The neural network architecture (`NiftyLSTMForecaster` in `src/model.py`) consists of the following precisely parameterized components:

• Input Dimension: d_{in} = 11 (Close_rel, High_rel, Low_rel, Open_rel, SMA10_rel, SMA30_rel, EMA20_rel, RSI_norm, Return, Range, Vol_norm).

• Recurrent Core: Stacked 2-Layer LSTM with hidden dimension d_{hid} = 64 (5m model) or 128 (daily model). Inter-layer dropout is configured at 0.20 to prevent co-adaptation of recurrent units.

• Sequence Memory: L = 30 intervals for intraday (2.5 hours of 5-minute memory); L = 60 intervals for daily (3 months of macroeconomic memory).

• Dense Projection Decoder: The final hidden state h_L in R^{d_{hid}} is passed through a multi-stage non-linear projection head: Dense(d_{hid} -> 128) -> LayerNorm(128) -> LeakyReLU(alpha=0.1) -> Dropout(0.15) -> Dense(128 -> 64) -> LeakyReLU(alpha=0.1) -> Dense(64 -> H * 4).

• Output Tensor Reshaping: The output vector is reshaped into R^{batch_size, H, 4}, producing simultaneous multi-horizon predictions for High, Low, Close, and Open across all H = 6 future steps.

4.3 Objective Function: Huber Loss (Smooth L1) Formulation

Financial time series contain frequent outliers resulting from macroeconomic announcements, corporate earnings surprises, and geopolitical events. Training with standard Mean Squared Error (MSE = (1/N) * sum (y - ^y)^2) causes gradients to scale quadratically with errors, leading to severe weight distortion when market spikes occur.

Conversely, Mean Absolute Error (MAE = (1/N) * sum |y - ^y|) maintains constant gradients even for tiny errors, resulting in slow convergence near local minima.

To obtain the optimal balance of quadratic convergence for small errors and linear robustness for outliers, this project implements the Huber Loss (Smooth L1 Loss) with threshold delta = 0.001: L_delta(y, ^y) = 0.5 * (y - ^y)^2 for |y - ^y| <= delta L_delta(y, ^y) = delta * (|y - ^y| - 0.5 * delta) for |y - ^y| > delta This loss function provides exceptional numerical stability and convergence on relative percentage return targets.

4.4 Optimization Strategy: AdamW & Plateau Learning Rate Decay

Optimization is performed using AdamW (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019), which decouples weight decay from gradient-based momentum updates: theta_{t+1} = theta_t - eta_t * (m_t / (sqrt(v_t) + epsilon)) - eta_t * lambda * theta_t, where lambda = 1e-4 is the weight decay regularization coefficient, avoiding overfitting on recurrent weight matrices.

Learning rate scheduling utilizes `ReduceLROnPlateau` monitoring validation loss with a patience factor of 4 epochs and decay multiplier of 0.5. Gradient clipping (`torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_`) with a maximum norm threshold of 1.0 is enforced across all backward passes to prevent exploding gradients.

4.5 Elimination of Lookahead Bias (Chronological Data Splitting)

A catastrophic error in academic and industrial time series literature is the application of randomized k-fold cross-validation or random train-test splitting. In financial time series, random shuffling introduces fatal lookahead bias (data leakage), where future price information leaks into past feature sequences.

To guarantee complete methodological integrity, this project enforces strict chronological splitting: • Training Split: First 85% of continuous chronological records. • Validation Split: Subsequent 7.5% of records (used strictly for early stopping and checkpoint selection). • Unseen Test Split: Final 7.5% of records (completely withheld from training and validation, evaluated only once for empirical reporting).

CHAPTER 5: FULL-STACK WEB APPLICATION & REST API ARCHITECTURE

5.1 FastAPI Backend Engine & Asynchronous Architecture

The production backend server (`server.py`) is engineered using FastAPI, a modern, high-performance web framework for Python leveraging ASGI standards and Starlette. It runs on the Uvicorn ASGI server on port 8000.

FastAPI was selected over traditional Flask frameworks due to its native asynchronous concurrency (`async/await`), automatic OpenAPI/Swagger schema documentation, and type-validated Pydantic models. An in-memory LRU caching layer caches raw predictions for 120 seconds, allowing hundreds of concurrent client connections without redundant neural network forward passes.

5.2 REST API Endpoint Specifications & Data Contracts

6.3 Quantitative Performance Metrics (RMSE, MAE, MAPE, R²)

Model performance was evaluated on the completely withheld chronological test split using four standard regression metrics:

1. Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE): Measures the square root of mean squared prediction errors, penalizing large outliers: RMSE = sqrt( (1/N) * sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - ^y_i)^2 )

2. Mean Absolute Error (MAE): Measures the average magnitude of absolute errors in rupee terms: MAE = (1/N) * sum_{i=1}^N |y_i - ^y_i|

3. Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE): Expresses accuracy as an invariant relative percentage error: MAPE = (100 / N) * sum_{i=1}^N |(y_i - ^y_i) / y_i|

4. Coefficient of Determination (R² Score): Quantifies the proportion of variance in actual prices predictable by the model: R² = 1 - ( sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - ^y_i)^2 / sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - y_mean)^2 )

9.2 Authoritative Viva-Voce Questions & Answers (25 Comprehensive Entries)

**Q1: What is the primary objective of Experiment 13?**
A1: To design, train, and evaluate a deep recurrent neural network (Stacked LSTM) for multi-step time series forecasting of NIFTY-50 index High and Low boundaries, demonstrating mastery of sequential modeling under Course Outcome CO4.
**Q2: Why is stock market time series forecasting considered non-stationary?**
A2: Because its statistical properties (mean, variance, autocovariance) change over time due to economic cycles, policy shifts, and volatility clustering. Naive normalization leads to predictive failure.
**Q3: What causes the Vanishing Gradient Problem in standard vanilla RNNs?**
A3: Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) requires repeated matrix multiplication of the recurrent weight matrix. When eigenvalues are less than 1, gradients decay exponentially as sequence length increases, preventing long-term learning.
**Q4: How does the LSTM architecture solve the vanishing gradient problem?**
A4: By introducing a linear Memory Cell State (C_t) governed by a Constant Error Carousel (CEC). Information is updated via addition rather than repeated multiplication, allowing gradients to flow backward indefinitely without exponential decay.
**Q5: What are the three gates in an LSTM cell, and what are their equations?**
A5: Forget Gate: f_t = sigma(W_f[h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_f); Input Gate: i_t = sigma(W_i[h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_i); Output Gate: o_t = sigma(W_o[h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_o).
**Q6: What is the purpose of the candidate cell state (~C_t)?**
A6: It computes new potential memory information using tanh activation (~C_t = tanh(W_c[h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_c)), which is modulated by input gate i_t and added into cell state C_t.
**Q7: What is the difference between an LSTM and a GRU?**
A7: A GRU combines forget and input gates into a single Update Gate, merges cell and hidden states into a single state, and uses approximately 25% fewer parameters. LSTMs maintain superior long-range memory retention on complex multi-step series.
**Q8: Why is direct multi-horizon forecasting preferred over recursive forecasting?**
A8: Recursive forecasting feeds predicted outputs back into the input for subsequent steps, causing errors to compound exponentially. Direct multi-horizon forecasting projects all H future steps simultaneously from the final hidden state.
**Q9: What is 'mean-reversion flatline failure' in financial deep learning?**
A9: When trained on globally normalized absolute prices, minor intraday price changes become infinitesimally small in normalized space. The network minimizes MSE by predicting the sequence mean, causing all future candles to look flat.
**Q10: How does the Relative-Offset Formulation solve this flatline failure?**
A10: By normalizing all window features and target horizons relative to the sequence's final Close price P_0 (Price_rel = Price / P_0 - 1). This yields a stationary, zero-mean distribution that allows the LSTM to learn genuine momentum and spreads.
**Q11: Why is chronological data splitting mandatory in financial time series?**
A11: Random shuffling introduces lookahead bias (data leakage), where future price information leaks into past feature sequences, producing artificially inflated metrics that collapse during live deployment.
**Q12: Why was Huber Loss selected over Mean Squared Error (MSE)?**
A12: Financial data contains heavy-tailed outliers. MSE squares errors, leading to gradient explosion on spikes. Huber loss is quadratic for small errors (|e| <= delta) and linear for large errors, combining convergence speed with outlier robustness.
**Q13: What role does the AdamW optimizer play in this architecture?**
A13: AdamW decouples weight decay (L2 regularization) from gradient momentum updates, preventing weight magnitude growth and improving generalization on unseen test splits.
**Q14: Explain the mathematical calculation of the Relative Strength Index (RSI).**
A14: RSI = 100 - (100 / (1 + RS)), where RS is the ratio of smoothed 14-period average upward price changes to smoothed 14-period downward price changes. Values above 70 indicate overbought conditions; values below 30 indicate oversold.
**Q15: How does the system handle exchange trading hours (09:15 to 15:30 IST)?**
A15: Through a specialized calendar rollover module (`get_next_5m_intervals`). When time exceeds 15:30, it rolls to 09:15 AM of the next business day, skipping Saturdays, Sundays, and market holidays.
**Q16: What is free-float market capitalization, and how does it relate to NIFTY-50?**
A16: It weights index constituents based on the market value of shares readily available for public trading (excluding promoter lock-ins). NIFTY-50 uses this weighting across 50 leading Indian equities.
**Q17: What was the root cause of the raw HTML tags leaking in the Streamlit UI?**
A17: Indenting multiline HTML strings by 4 or more spaces inside nested Python columns triggered CommonMark's markdown code-block parser. Replacing raw HTML with native `st.container(border=True)` and `st.metric()` resolved the defect.
**Q18: What is Layer Normalization, and why is it used in the dense projection head?**
A18: Unlike Batch Normalization which normalizes across the batch dimension, LayerNorm normalizes across the feature dimension for each sample independently, stabilizing hidden state dynamics in sequential networks.
**Q19: What is the Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) achieved by the 5m model?**
A19: An exceptional High Price MAPE of 0.05% and Low Price MAPE of 0.05%, corresponding to an average error of only ~Rs. 11 on the NIFTY index at 22,936.
**Q20: What is the coefficient of determination (R²) achieved by the daily model?**
A20: An R² score of 0.9625 on the completely withheld test split spanning 2025–2026, confirming high predictive fidelity.
**Q21: Where are the trained model weights stored on disk?**
A21: `C:\Users\hp\Nifty-50_predictor\models\nifty50_lstm_5m.pth` (5-minute model) and `C:\Users\hp\Nifty-50_predictor\models\nifty50_lstm.pth` (daily model).
**Q22: How does the unified chart render historical and predicted data simultaneously?**
A22: In Plotly.js, historical candles and predicted candles are plotted as continuous traces along the same x-axis, separated by a vertical dashed demarcation line indicating the current transition timestamp.
**Q23: How does the system ensure safety during software upgrades?**
A23: Through a dual-layer savepoint mechanism: Git version tags (`savepoint_v1`, `savepoint_v2`) and an independent physical mirror in `_backups/checkpoint_v1/`, backed by a 1-click `rollback_to_v1.bat` script.
**Q24: What is the difference between univariate and multivariate time series forecasting?**
A24: Univariate forecasting uses only past values of the target variable. Multivariate forecasting (implemented here) uses multiple synchronized features: Open, High, Low, Close, Volume, SMAs, EMA, RSI, and Intraday Range.
